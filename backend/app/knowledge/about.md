# About Sanskar Agrawal

## Who I Am
I'm Sanskar Agrawal, from Indore, India. I'm a final-year Computer Science and Engineering student at Acropolis Institute of Technology and Research, with a deep focus on AI engineering and full-stack development.

## My Story
In school, I was always curious about how things work under the hood — like how Google can instantly tell the difference between someone searching for a fruit "apple" and an "Apple" phone. That curiosity slowly turned into a real passion, which is why I pursued Computer Science and Engineering.

In my first year of college, I was honestly a bit lost about which direction to take. Then I participated in a hackathon called Y Path with a small team, and we won it. That moment changed things for me — I realized one of my real strengths is structured thinking: taking a messy, ambiguous problem and breaking it down until it's solvable. That win gave me a clear direction — I wanted to build things, especially with AI.

## What Drives Me
I care more about solving real problems than collecting technologies. I don't stop at "it works" — I push until something is production-ready: tested, reliable, and able to survive real-world use, not just a demo.

## Strengths

- End-to-end ownership — comfortable owning a project from architecture to deployment, solo
- AI + product thinking — not just building with AI tools, but identifying real problems AI can solve
- Fast execution under pressure (hackathon-tested)

## Growth Area
For a long time, I struggled with explaining my work clearly, especially under pressure — I was much stronger at building than at presenting. I deliberately started taking on roles in hackathons and events where pitching and explaining ideas to judges was unavoidable, specifically to fix this. It's still a work in progress, but there's a clear improvement compared to a year ago.

## 10-Year Vision

I want to build or lead a product that does this at real scale — not a handful of users, but millions, maybe billions — proving that decisions about people should be based on what they've actually done, not how well they've gamed a filter.
